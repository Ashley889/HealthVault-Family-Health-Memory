# Nura — Mobile App Enhancement

### Tagline

**Your family's health, remembered.**

Enhance the **existing Nura mobile app prototype**. Do not redesign the product from scratch. Keep the existing design system, visual language, colors, typography, cards, spacing, and overall UX direction because the current design system is good.

The goal is to make the product **more complete, functional, information-rich, and genuinely usable as a mobile application on both Android and iOS.**

---

# 1. Mobile-First Compatibility — HIGH PRIORITY

This must be a **mobile application experience**, not a desktop/web dashboard.

Design and optimize all screens for:

* Android phones
* iPhones
* Different screen sizes
* Small and large mobile screens
* Portrait orientation primarily
* Responsive layouts

The UI must never:

* Cut off text
* Hide important information
* Crop cards
* Overflow horizontally
* Cut buttons
* Hide bottom navigation
* Create unusable scrolling areas

Use responsive mobile layouts and allow content to scroll naturally when there is more information.

Do NOT optimize the design around laptop/desktop screens.

---

# 2. Medication Information — MUST BE EXPANDED

The current app does not show enough information about medications.

Medication is a core part of a person's health history.

Whenever a doctor prescribes medication, capture:

### Medication

* Medicine name
* Dosage
* Frequency
* Time of day
* Start date
* End date, if applicable
* Why it was prescribed
* Doctor who prescribed it
* Related health issue / journey
* Instructions
* Current status

Status should be:

**Active**

**Completed**

**Stopped**

---

## Medication Detail Screen

Example:

### Thyroxine 50 mcg

**Prescribed by**
Dr. Sharma

**Reason**
Thyroid management

**Dosage**
50 mcg

**Frequency**
Once daily

**Time**
Morning

**Started**
June 10, 2026

**End date**
Ongoing

**Instructions**
As prescribed by doctor

**Related journey**
Thyroid

This information should be visible from:

**Family Member → Health Journey → Medications**

and also from:

**Family Member → Health Overview → Current Medications**

---

# 3. Medication History

Do not show only the current medication.

Users should be able to understand:

### Current Medications

Active medications.

### Previous Medications

Medications that were completed or stopped.

Example:

**Medication A**
June 2026 – Present
Active

**Medication B**
March 2026 – May 2026
Completed

This creates a useful medication history over time.

---

# 4. Reminder Flow — IMPORTANT

The current reminder flow is too simple.

A reminder being created does NOT mean that the task/appointment actually happened.

Every reminder should have a lifecycle.

### Reminder statuses:

**Upcoming**

**Completed**

**Missed**

**Rescheduled**

---

## Example

### Mom's Doctor Appointment

**September 25**

Status:

**Upcoming**

After the date:

Ask:

### “What happened?”

Options:

**Appointment completed**

**Appointment missed**

**Appointment rescheduled**

---

## If Appointment Completed

Show:

### Appointment Completed

Ask:

**What happened during the visit?**

Allow the user to add:

* Doctor
* Reason for visit
* Doctor's notes
* Diagnosis / health issue
* Medication prescribed
* Tests requested
* Follow-up date
* Upload prescription/report

CTA:

### Save Visit

This should automatically create a new entry in the person's health timeline.

---

## If Appointment Missed

Show:

**Why was it missed?**

Optional note.

Then:

### Reschedule

---

## If Rescheduled

Allow:

**New date**

**New time**

Then the reminder becomes:

**Rescheduled**

This makes reminders part of the actual health journey rather than isolated calendar notifications.

---

This is important.

# 10. Health Journey Enhancement

Each long-term health journey should contain four clear sections:

### Overview

Short summary.

### Timeline

Everything that happened chronologically.

### Medications

Current + previous medication history.

### Reports

All reports related to this health issue.

Also show:

**Doctors**

**Appointments**

**Follow-ups**

This gives every long-term issue its own complete story.

---

# 11. Short-Term Issues

Short-term problems should remain lightweight.

Example:

### Left Shoulder Pain

**Started:** September 18

**Status:** Ongoing

**Doctor:** Dr. Sharma

**Medication:** Pain relief medication

**Test:** X-ray

**Follow-up:** September 25

When recovered:

### Mark as Resolved

The issue moves to:

**Past Health Issues**

but remains permanently available in the timeline.

# 14. Responsive Design Requirements

Every screen must be tested conceptually across:

### Small Android

360 × 800

### Standard Android

390 × 844

### iPhone

393 × 852

### Larger phone

430 × 932

Make sure:

* Text wraps naturally
* Cards expand vertically
* Buttons remain accessible
* Bottom navigation stays visible
* No content is clipped
* Long medical names do not break the layout
* Reports and medication information can scroll
* Long timelines remain usable

---

# 15. Overall UX Principle

Do not remove information just to make the screen look clean.

Instead:

> **Show the most important information first and allow the user to expand for more detail.**

For example:

### Medication

**Thyroxine 50 mcg**
50 mcg • Once daily • Active

Tap →

Full medication details.

Same principle for:

* Reports
* Doctor visits
* Health journeys
* Appointments
* Family members
